Water damage can be a serious problem for homeowners. It is not just about visible damage, such as discolored walls or warped floors. Homeowner guidance

Water Damage Prevention and Response Tips

Early detection, appropriate professional advice and regular home maintenance can help homeowners reduce the risk of water-related problems.

01

Act Quickly When You Find Water

The longer water remains, the more opportunity it has to affect your home's structure and contents. If you notice water intrusion, identify and stop the source only if it is safe to do so, and arrange an appropriate assessment.

02

Check for Hidden Water Damage After Storms

Even if your home appears dry after a storm, water can seep behind walls or beneath floors. Watch for damp areas, staining, changes in flooring and musty odors.

03

Address Moisture to Reduce Mold Risk

Mold can develop in persistently damp environments. After water damage, the affected areas need to be dried appropriately, including spaces where moisture may not be immediately visible.

04

Inspect Your Basement Regularly

Basements can be vulnerable to water intrusion, particularly after heavy rain or flooding. Look for dampness, leaks, water stains and musty smells, and investigate changes promptly.

05

Know the Signs of Water Damage

Discolored walls, peeling paint, musty odors and buckled flooring can indicate moisture problems. Do not ignore changes simply because the source of the water is not obvious.

06

Seal Gaps Around Windows and Doors

Inspect and maintain seals around windows, doors and the foundation. Small openings can allow water to enter during storms and may contribute to costly repairs.

07

Be Careful With DIY Drying

Surface drying alone may not remove moisture trapped in building materials. Inadequate drying can leave problems behind, so consider professional guidance when water has affected walls, floors or structural areas.

08

Keep Up With Preventative Maintenance

Regularly check your roof, plumbing and drainage systems. Addressing small maintenance issues early can reduce the chance of more extensive water intrusion later.
